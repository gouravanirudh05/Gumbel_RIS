Important Plot Descriptions
===========================

Metric used in the main plots
-----------------------------
The main performance metric is the relative improvement of MC-aware optimization
over the standard no-coupling optimization when both are evaluated with mutual
coupling:

Improvement (%) = 100 * (Gain_MC-aware_with_MC - Gain_standard_with_MC)
                  / Gain_standard_with_MC

This metric is preferred over raw average channel gain because it directly shows
the benefit of accounting for mutual coupling under the real coupled channel.


1. 01_mcaware_relative_improvement_vs_spacing.png
-------------------------------------------------
This is the most important plot for explaining the spacing trend.

The x-axis shows the RIS inter-element spacing: lambda/4, lambda/2, and lambda.
The y-axis shows the percentage improvement from MC-aware optimization. The
three panels correspond to N_RIS = 16, 64, and 256.

Key observation:
MC-aware optimization gives the largest gain at lambda/4 spacing, especially
for N_RIS = 256. For the largest RIS, the improvements are approximately:

Greedy:      10.09%
Gumbel:      12.31%
Transformer: 13.95%

As spacing increases to lambda/2 and lambda, the improvement drops sharply and
approaches zero. This supports the expected physical behavior that mutual
coupling is strongest when elements are closely spaced and becomes weak for
larger spacing.


2. 02_mcaware_relative_improvement_vs_nris.png
----------------------------------------------
This plot is best for showing how RIS size affects the usefulness of MC-aware
optimization.

The x-axis shows the number of RIS elements: 16, 64, and 256. The y-axis shows
the MC-aware relative improvement in percent. The three panels correspond to
lambda/4, lambda/2, and lambda spacing.

Key observation:
At lambda/4 spacing, the improvement increases strongly with N_RIS. The gain is
almost negligible for N_RIS = 16, modest for N_RIS = 64, and large for
N_RIS = 256. This indicates that mutual coupling becomes more important in
large, dense RIS arrays.

At lambda/2 and lambda spacing, increasing N_RIS does not produce the same
large improvement. This shows that spacing is the dominant factor controlling
the coupling effect.


3. 03_raw_channel_gain_vs_nris_lambda_4.png
------------------------------------------
This plot shows the raw average channel gain versus the number of RIS elements
at lambda/4 spacing, where mutual coupling is strongest.

It includes the standard no-coupling reference, the standard design evaluated
with mutual coupling, and the MC-aware design evaluated with mutual coupling
for all three approaches.

Key observation:
This plot is useful as a supporting figure, but the raw channel gain values are
harder to compare directly across RIS sizes because different N_RIS settings may
have different absolute channel scaling and normalization effects. Therefore,
the relative-improvement plots should be used as the main paper figures, while
this raw-gain plot can be used as supplementary evidence.


Overall conclusion
------------------
The results show that MC-aware optimization is most beneficial for large,
densely packed RIS deployments. The strongest gains occur at lambda/4 spacing
and N_RIS = 256. As the spacing increases to lambda/2 and lambda, the advantage
of MC-aware optimization becomes small, indicating that the coupled channel
approaches the no-coupling behavior for widely spaced RIS elements.
